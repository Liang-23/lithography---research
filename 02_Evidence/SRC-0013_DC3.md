### Completion and correction mechanism

- DC3 addresses

$$
\min_y f_x(y)
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
g_x(y)\le 0,
\qquad
h_x(y)=0.
$$

[PDF p. 3, Eq. (1), Section 3]
- The network outputs partial variables $z$. Equality completion $\phi_x(z)$ fills the remaining variables, explicitly or through an implicit solve, to satisfy $h_x(y)=0$. Inequality correction then takes gradient steps on squared violation with respect to the partial variables while recomputing completion. [PDF pp. 3–5, Section 3.1, Algorithm 1, Eqs. (3)–(4), Section 3.2]
- Equality completion depends on the assumption that selected partial variables determine a valid completion. Inequality correction is guaranteed for linear constraints; for general nonlinear or nonconvex constraints the authors state only a practical expectation when initialization is near feasible. [PDF p. 5, Section 3.2]

### Training, gradients, and inference

- Completion and correction run during both training and testing. Training uses

$$
\ell_{\mathrm{soft}}
=f_x(\hat y)
+\lambda_g\lVert\operatorname{ReLU}(g_x(\hat y))\rVert_2^2
+\lambda_h\lVert h_x(\hat y)\rVert_2^2.
$$

[PDF p. 3, Eq. (2); PDF p. 4, Algorithm 1]
- Completion is differentiated explicitly or by the implicit function theorem; inequality correction is unrolled. Reported experiments use $t_{\mathrm{train}}=t_{\mathrm{test}}=10$ for QP/nonconvex tasks and $5$ for ACOPF. [PDF pp. 4–6, Sections 3.1–4]
- DC3 requires no optimal solver labels. No corrected output is stored as a target or transferred to a second student; the `Eq. NN` solver-supervised comparator is a separate baseline. [PDF pp. 3 and 6]
- Test-time inference always includes equality completion and inequality correction. Depending on the problem, completion can require an implicit numerical solve and correction requires repeated gradient steps. There is no gate or fallback optimizer. [PDF p. 4, Algorithm 1; PDF p. 6, baseline discussion]

### Output dimensionality and reported results

- Synthetic tasks use $100$ decision variables, $50$ equality constraints, and $50$ inequalities. ACOPF uses a 57-node system with load inputs and generation/complex-voltage outputs. These are moderate vector or graph-state outputs, not dense images or spatial tensors. [PDF pp. 7–9, Eq. (5), Tables 1–3]
- On the convex QP, DC3 reports objective $-13.46$ versus optimizer $-15.05$, printed violations $0.00$, and time $0.017\,\mathrm{s}$ versus qpth $1.335\,\mathrm{s}$ and OSQP $0.002\,\mathrm{s}$. [PDF p. 7, Table 1]
- On ACOPF, DC3 reports objective $3.82$ versus PYPOWER $3.81$ and time $0.089\,\mathrm{s}$ versus $0.949\,\mathrm{s}$. The prose qualifies the rounded-zero table entries: three of 100 cases have slight violations and negative optimality gaps. [PDF p. 9, Table 3 and final paragraph of Section 4.3]
- Ablations show that training-time correction matters on the QP and simple nonconvex tasks; ACOPF is less sensitive in the reported setting. [PDF pp. 7–9, Tables 1–3]

### Limitations and safe boundary

- Completion and correction cost is problem-dependent; implicit completion can be expensive and correction convergence varies. Timing comparisons combine different hardware and implementations. [PDF pp. 6 and 9, Sections 4–5]
- Higher correction learning rates were often unstable and hyperparameters were selected partly through informal experimentation. [PDF pp. 12–13, Appendix B]
- General nonlinear correction has no general convergence guarantee, global optimality is not guaranteed, and multi-solution selection is not studied. No correction-distance bound, supervised damage analysis, OOD study, gate, or fallback is provided.
- **Bounded relevance:** direct prior art for separating a neural partial prediction from differentiable equality completion and terminal inequality correction. The corrector remains at deployment and no corrected-target knowledge transfer occurs.

- **Retained takeaway:** the network can predict a subset of solution variables, and known equations can calculate the remaining variables. This requires a valid, computable completion relationship for the selected subset; the network is not discovering unknown physical equations. [PDF pp. 3–4, Section 3.1]
- **Retained takeaway:** optimal-answer labels need not be supplied when the task provides a computable optimization objective and known constraints. The learner needs both a way to evaluate candidate solutions and a statement of what those solutions must satisfy. This is the constrained-optimization setting in the paper, not evidence that arbitrary reference-image prediction can dispense with labels. [PDF p. 3, Eqs. (1)–(2), PDF p. 4, Algorithm 1]
- **Implementation boundary:** DC3 retains equality completion and inequality correction at inference, and uses objective-plus-constraint loss in training. General nonlinear finite-step correction and global optimality are not guaranteed. [PDF pp. 4–6]
